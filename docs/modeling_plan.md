# First modeling stage: target and validation design

## Current data-stage decision (2026-09-30)

The complete GeoMet sample-level analysis is reproducible, but its observed schema still lacks collars/surveys, interval support, geology domains, density, a documented CRS/vertical datum, and assay QA metadata. A separate screen of public drillhole sources identified the Northern Territory Geological Survey's DIP001 as a promising real-data candidate: the official record lists collar, downhole-survey, drillhole-sample assay, raw-repeat, and laboratory-metadata products under CC BY 4.0. The advertised archive is 1.04 GB; its actual files and columns have not been inspected, and no standardized lithology-interval table is listed. See [dataset screening](dataset_screening.md).

Do not adapt the current GeoMet model scripts to DIP001 until a coherent project subset passes the schema, join, interval, coordinate, geology, and QA/QC gate documented there. If that gate is unavailable, retain the current empirical study as a sample-level geostatistical/prediction analysis and stop short of a block model or mineral-resource claim.

## Stage gate

Do not fit or publish an estimate until the complete `drillholes.csv` has been downloaded and the full-file QA/QC report exists. The Zenodo preview confirms the 22-column header, but it is only a preview and does not support full-data statistics, variography, or model validation.

## Provisional target

Use `Cu ppm` as the first univariate target, subject to full-data checks for missingness, values at reporting/detection limits, extreme skew, and duplicate sample coordinates. It is a chemical concentration target, not a declared mineral resource grade or economic estimate. Preserve ppm as the native unit initially. Consider Au/Ag/S as secondary targets only after the copper workflow is defensible.

## Validation design

1. Inspect per-hole sample counts and spatial distribution.
2. Hold out whole `HOLEID` groups (GroupKFold or leave-one-hole-out), never random rows. This tests transfer to unsampled trajectories and reduces leakage from adjacent samples on the same hole.
3. Compare a mean baseline, nearest-neighbour, and IDW before fitting ordinary kriging.
4. Report MAE, RMSE, mean error, and observed-versus-predicted plots by fold; include fold-level spread rather than only pooled scores.
5. Fit variograms and all transformations using training data within each fold. Check directional continuity and anisotropy before selecting a 3D variogram.
6. Compare kriging predictions and kriging variance only after checking variogram fit and spatial coverage. Do not interpret kriging variance alone as a complete resource uncertainty statement.

## Why this sequence

The data contains sample-centroid coordinates and hole identifiers rather than documented collar, survey, and interval tables. A grouped validation and restrained language are appropriate to the available support. A 3D block model, tonnage, cut-off grade, classification, or mine plan would require additional geological, density, survey, domain, and economic inputs.

## Completed first baseline

The file is present locally and its MD5 matches Zenodo. QA/QC found 2,000 rows, 22 columns, 119 hole IDs, no missing values, no exact duplicate rows, and no duplicate coordinate triplets. The leave-one-hole-out baseline found IDW (p=2, k=12) MAE 5,173.6 ppm and RMSE 7,561.9 ppm, compared with training-mean MAE 5,314.2 ppm and RMSE 7,734.0 ppm. See `reports/initial_qaqc_summary.md` for the full summary and the caveats.

## Immediate next modeling action

The first fixed-k buffered ordinary-kriging experiment did not beat the buffered mean. A nested kriging sensitivity modestly improved overall scores but retained shrinkage at high Cu. Two nested ridge variants using all 17 companion assays achieved MAE/RMSE 1,795/2,897 ppm (alpha=0.1–100) and 1,898/3,248 ppm (alpha=100–10,000); the S/Ag/Au variant scored 2,075/3,593 ppm, versus 5,353/7,804 ppm for the same-split mean. The two all-assay variants use identical predictors and folds but different regularization search ranges and are preserved separately. These assume companion assays are known at held-out locations and are conditional geochemical prediction, not estimation where all assays are absent. See `reports/geochem_covariate_cv.json` and `reports/geochem_feature_sensitivity.json`. The dataset has no geology/domain or sample interval fields; related test tables are catalogued in provenance but not analyzed.

The observed drillhole file has no missing assay values, so there is no empirical missingness mechanism to emulate. A target-location dropout stress test found that removing Ag raised overall MAE to 2,734 ppm; removing all three S/Ag/Au raised it to 3,866 ppm and top-quartile MAE to 7,650 ppm. These are hypothetical masks, not observed patterns. The associated comminution and flotation tables are checksum-verified and locally audited, but no exact sample-level join to drillhole assays is established. A separate nested leave-one-hole-out LCT pilot (52 rows) did not beat the mean baseline. A nearest/second-nearest same-hole analysis finds apparently unique geometry for many rows, but cannot establish sample identity or interval support (`reports/companion_link_sensitivity.json`). Next, obtain the source crosswalk, coordinate reference/survey method, and support/compositing definitions before using lab responses as labels. To pursue geology-aware estimation, obtain lithology/domain, sample intervals, and assay QA metadata first. Do not report tonnage, classification, or economic value from this dataset alone.

## Spatial support audit (completed)

The new audit (`reports/spatial_support_audit.json`, reproduced by `python scripts/spatial_support_audit.py`) summarizes local coordinate support before any grid prediction. Of 48 possible 300-unit XY tiles in the bounding grid, 22 contain samples. The median nearest 3D sample spacing is 15.9 coordinate units; the median distance to the nearest sample from a different hole is 57.3 units. These figures describe the supplied sampling geometry only. They are not uncertainty estimates, and coordinate units/CRS remain undocumented. A future estimate should be restricted to a justified sampled domain and reported with nearest-data support diagnostics; the current data cannot define that domain or support an extrapolation claim.

## Support-limited interpolation prototype (completed)

`python scripts/copper_idw_grid.py` creates a 50-unit 3D IDW grid (p=2, k=12) for Cu ppm. Candidate nodes are limited to the XY convex hull and observed Z range, then screened by nearest-sample distance (at most the observed Q3, 21.85 units) and 12th-neighbor distance (at most 2x the median nearest-different-hole spacing, 114.54 units). Only 366 of 9,270 candidate nodes passed (3.95%); rejected nodes are omitted. This thin supported coverage is a warning against interpreting a continuous volume from the current coordinates. The polygon can still bridge geological gaps, the distance thresholds are provisional screening rules, and IDW has no geological domains or anisotropy. The resulting CSV and method metadata are `outputs/copper_idw_grid.csv` and `reports/copper_idw_grid.json`; these predictions are exploratory concentrations, not block estimates or resource confidence.

Next, review a section/map of accepted nodes and source geology/sample intervals. Do not densify the grid or relax the support cutoffs merely to make a smoother-looking volume. The geological boundary and support inputs must come from documented source data.

### Support-cutoff sensitivity and holdout check

The candidate-node coverage is sensitive to distance limits: across the tested combinations in `reports/grid_support_sensitivity.json`, coverage ranges from 0.5% to 34.1%. More importantly, buffered spatial holdout validation (`reports/grid_support_validation.json`) shows that the current grid's distance criteria have no matching held-out sample cases at all (0/2,000); they were based on densely sampled full-data nearest-neighbor spacing, while validation excludes nearby training data. Only the loosest tested band evaluated as many as 50 held-out points (2.5%), far too few to choose a cutoff or claim reliable performance. On all 2,000 buffered holdouts, IDW MAE/RMSE is 5,479/8,046 ppm versus training-mean 5,353/7,804 ppm, so IDW did not outperform the mean on this split. Keep the 366-node grid explicitly exploratory; do not interpret its support screen as empirically validated. Next methodological work should calibrate support rules on an adequately sized spatial validation set and use source geological boundaries/support data before revising any grid.

The full holdout distance-decile diagnostic (`reports/support_error_calibration.json` and `.svg`) does not show a stable monotonic increase in Cu MAE as either nearest-sample or 12th-neighbor distance grows. The farthest decile has lower MAE than several middle deciles, and the mean baseline wins some distance bands while IDW wins others. This indicates that geometric distance alone does not define prediction quality in this dataset. Treat distance as a support descriptor, not an uncertainty proxy; a defensible threshold needs geology-aware validation and enough independent held-out spatial domains.

### Compositional-data readiness (completed)

Before adopting log-ratio transforms from a published GeoMet workflow, `scripts/composition_readiness.py` checked the observed assay totals. The 18 reported analytes sum to a median 361,988 ppm (range 42,439–616,630 ppm); none of 2,000 rows is near 1,000,000 ppm. Thus they do not constitute a closed whole-rock composition. The selected Cu-Au-Ag-S subcomposition has a median sum of 6,801 ppm and includes zeros in seven rows (Cu zero in four, Au zero in seven); the source does not state whether zeros are below-detection values or true zeros. A CLR could only be interpreted as a four-part subcomposition, and it would require a justified zero-replacement sensitivity analysis. Do not describe it as whole-rock mass fractions. Full results are in `reports/composition_readiness.json`.

An exploratory four-part spatial sensitivity is now in `reports/subcomposition_spatial_sensitivity.json`. It replaces zeros with 0.1x or 0.5x each component's minimum positive training-fold value, closes Cu/Au/Ag/S, IDW-interpolates each CLR coordinate and log subcomposition total (p=2, k=12), then reconstructs Cu ppm. Under leave-one-hole-out, MAE improves to 4,723 ppm from raw-Cu IDW's 5,174, but high-grade MAE rises to 10,696 from 9,142 ppm and mean error is -1,571 ppm. Under buffered spatial tiles, MAE is 5,146 versus 5,479 raw IDW and 5,353 training mean, while RMSE is 8,192 versus 8,046 IDW and 7,804 mean; high-grade MAE is 12,637 versus 11,105 IDW and 10,543 mean, with mean error -2,539 ppm. The two arbitrary replacement amounts yield nearly identical scores, but this does not validate them because the true detection limits are unknown. Overall MAE gains therefore do not establish a reliable spatial model; high-grade shrinkage and negative bias are unacceptable for resource interpretation. This is only an exploratory subcomposition, not whole-rock composition or resource estimation.

## Declustering mean baselines (completed)

Equal-hole and equal-occupied-300-unit-XY-tile mean baselines were added to test whether the sample-weighted mean hides clustering (`reports/declustering_baselines.json`). Means are calculated from training data only per fold. In buffered XY-tile validation, sample-weighted mean MAE/RMSE is 5,353/7,804 ppm; equal-hole is 5,167/7,804; equal-tile is 5,044/7,838. The equal-tile mean reduces overall MAE but increases high-grade MAE (11,484 vs. 10,543 ppm for the sample mean) and introduces -1,023 ppm mean error. In leave-one-hole-out validation, equal-tile also lowers MAE (5,018 vs. 5,314) while raising RMSE (7,783 vs. 7,734) and high-grade MAE (11,347 vs. 10,412). These are reference means under different spatial weighting assumptions, not validated declustering weights or estimates. The result confirms that headline MAE depends on the target weighting and reinforces reporting multiple metrics and grade ranges.

## Grade-threshold screening (completed)

Buffered spatial holdout predictions were also evaluated as screens for samples above two descriptive Cu thresholds: the source-data top quartile (8,900 ppm) and top decile (18,100 ppm). These are distribution cutoffs, not economic thresholds or ore/waste classifications. At 8,900 ppm, nested kriging has balanced accuracy 0.565 and average precision 0.341; raw IDW has 0.536 and 0.300. At 18,100 ppm, raw IDW identifies only 2 of 202 high-Cu samples and nested kriging identifies none at that same prediction cutoff. The Cu-Au-Ag-S subcomposition has top-decile recall zero. The conditional all-assay ridge screen is much stronger (balanced accuracy 0.926 and average precision 0.943 at the top-decile threshold), but assumes 17 non-Cu assays are available at each held-out location. This is a conditional geochemical screen, not prediction into fully unassayed blocks. Full confusion counts and precision/recall metrics are in `reports/grade_threshold_classification.json`.

A top-ranked workload analysis (`reports/top_rank_screening.json`) asks how many high-Cu samples occur if only the top 5%, 10%, or 20% of each held-out tile's predictions are reviewed. The initial pooled ranking compared absolute scores across independently fitted folds and overstated capture rates due to fold-specific calibration shifts; it is retained only as a diagnostic. The fold-wise percentile ranking is the more defensible comparison: at a 20% review rate it captures 22.3% of observed top-decile samples with nested kriging, 19.3% with raw IDW, and 23.3% with subcomposition IDW. The alpha 0.1–100 conditional all-assay ridge captures 62.9% within its top 10% and 88.1% within its top 20%; alpha 100–10,000 captures 62.4% and 86.1%. Both assume all co-located assays are available. This is a ranking/workload sensitivity only: review fractions do not translate to economic decisions, and the conditional model is not deployable where assays are absent.

## Spatial-fold stability (completed)

A paired bootstrap resampled the 22 buffered XY tiles 10,000 times (`reports/spatial_fold_bootstrap.json`). For nested kriging minus the training mean, the median MAE difference is -149 ppm with a 95% tile-bootstrap percentile interval of -549 to +171 ppm; zero lies within the interval. Raw IDW's median MAE difference is +125 ppm (-283 to +461 ppm). The conditional all-assay ridge MAE difference is -3,549 ppm (-4,126 to -3,064 ppm), but the assay-availability caveat remains. Subcomposition IDW's median MAE difference is -208 ppm (-653 to +149 ppm), while its high-grade MAE difference is +2,113 ppm (+1,004 to +3,333 ppm). These are descriptive stability intervals from only 22 potentially dependent folds, not formal population confidence intervals. They reinforce that the spatial-only model improvement over the simple mean is uncertain, and that compositional IDW worsens high-grade performance.

## Validation design sensitivity (completed)

The fixed 3D IDW baseline was re-evaluated across nine XY tile/buffer combinations (`reports/spatial_cv_design_sensitivity.json`): tile widths 200, 300, and 500 coordinate units crossed with buffers 0, 50, and 100. All combinations evaluated 2,000 samples, with 40, 22, or 12 folds, respectively. IDW beat the training mean on MAE in 5/9 settings, but on RMSE in only 3/9; at the adopted 300/50 setting it loses on both. Larger buffers generally reduced IDW performance relative to the mean, though results were not strictly monotonic. This confirms validation-design sensitivity. The unit values are source-local and undocumented, so the grid-size sensitivity is relative only; the design cannot establish generalization to a real mine block scale.

### Residual spatial structure (completed)

`scripts/residual_spatial_autocorrelation.py` builds 3D k-nearest-neighbour graphs (k=4, 8, 12) and measures Moran's I for signed and absolute out-of-fold Cu residuals from nested kriging and conditional ridge models. Permutations are restricted within outer folds to preserve fold-specific residual distributions. Moran's I remains positive across all graph sizes and models; empirical p=0.002 is the lower resolution bound of 499 permutations, not a precise population p-value. The conditional models reduce signed-error clustering but do not remove it. Coordinate scale and geological domains are not documented, so this finding signals unresolved spatial structure without identifying its cause. See `reports/residual_spatial_autocorrelation.json` and `outputs/spatial_residual_autocorrelation.md`. Do not apply an unvalidated residual-correction surface.

### Proxy surface clipping (completed)

The public GeoMet worked example uses an XY convex-hull footprint and estimates a simple surface from the first sample point in each hole. Following that documented workflow, `scripts/clip_copper_grid_to_surface.py` used 119 first-row-per-hole points and 2D IDW (p=2, k=12) to estimate a proxy surface. Source row order is top-down in Z for 118/119 hole groups (one group has equal first/last Z), but the table has no surveyed collar or terrain field. The proxy removed 11 of 366 support-screened grid points above or on the estimated surface, leaving 355. This is a geometric/topographic screening step only; it does not create geological domains or fix the grid's unvalidated support. See `reports/surface_clip_audit.json` and `outputs/copper_idw_grid_below_proxy_surface.csv`.

## Spatial grid-origin sensitivity (completed)

`scripts/spatial_origin_sensitivity.py` holds the 300-unit buffered XY-tile design and fixed 3D IDW (p=2, k=12) and training-mean methods constant, then shifts the XY grid origin through nine combinations of 0, 100, and 200 source-coordinate units. All 2,000 samples were evaluated in every design. IDW MAE ranged from 5,248 to 5,763 ppm and beat the training mean in 2/9 origins; mean-baseline MAE ranged only from 5,344 to 5,372 ppm. This indicates that tile-origin choice materially affects IDW validation. Origins are not independent folds and should not be tuned to improve scores. See `outputs/spatial_origin_sensitivity.md` and its generated figure.
